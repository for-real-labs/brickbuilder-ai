## Design a model worth looking at

A build that passes every check is not yet a good model. Decide these before the first part, and write them as comments at the top of the script:

- **Identity and silhouette.** Two or three main masses with different heights, and a clear base. The subject should be recognizable at thumbnail size before any small detail is added.
- **One focal feature**, two supporting features, then small accents. Do not make every surface equally busy: leave quiet walls, roofs and body panels.
- **Colors by role.** A main body color, a contrasting trim, a roof or top color, one small accent, plus glass and metal. Use few colors on large surfaces and repeat each role across the model.
- **Depth.** Recess the glazing, let sills, cornices, canopies and roof edges project, and step a facade in or out by a stud. A one-stud offset makes a useful shadow when it has real support under it.
- **A few motifs**, used consistently and varied by what they do: arches, a masonry texture, flower boxes, a grille.

Build in modules: base first, then each floor or section, then roof, then details. Give each module a clear envelope and say what it stands on. A detail replaces the wall or roof cells it occupies; it is never pasted through them.

After the first render, ask: is the subject recognizable when small? Are base, body, openings and top in convincing proportion? Is there depth on every visible side? Could an ornament be removed to make the design clearer? More parts are not better design.
