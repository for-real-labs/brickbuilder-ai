## Spaceships and aircraft

Decide the scale first (a figure fits, a larger display model, or microscale), then two or three defining masses: crossed wings, a long engine pair, a broad cargo hull, a wedge with a raised bridge. For a named ship, keep the proportions that define that variant.

Build from the frame outward. Reserve the cockpit, the cargo space and the engines first, then the spine, nose, wing roots, wings, hull panels, service details and the landing gear or stand. A long wing or an engine out on a pod needs a bonded stack of plates or a braced frame at its root: a part is not attached because its outline touches the hull.

Use the shapes made for it: canopies and windscreens, printed instrument tiles, seats, wedge plates in left and right pairs, curved slopes, cylinders, cones and dishes for engines, grilles, bars and clips. Do not replace them all with rectangular plates.

Keep the main hull color dominant, with smaller roles for panel lines, markings, frame, canopy and exhaust. Put fine pipes and grilles in a few recessed service areas and leave the broad hull and wing surfaces quiet. Give it real depth: an inset cockpit, layered leading edges, thin outer wing edges, concentric engine nozzles. Design the underside and the stand with the same care as the top.
